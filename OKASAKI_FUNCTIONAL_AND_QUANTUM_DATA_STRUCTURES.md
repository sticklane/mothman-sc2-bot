### 3.1 Skew-Binary and Redundant Carry-Save Accumulator

#### Mathematical Foundation (Okasaki Chapter 9.2)
In standard binary ($w_k = 2^k, d_k \in \{0, 1\}$), incrementing $2^k - 1 = \mathtt{11\dots1}_2$ triggers a worst-case $O(n)$ ripple carry across all $k$ bits. Because a quantum circuit controlled on a superposition state must wait for the longest carry chain across all branches, every controlled increment/addition inside the 590-step Bernstein–Yang safe-`divsteps` loop suffers $O(n)$ (or $O(\sqrt{n}) = 32$) Toffoli depth per step.

In **Okasaki's Skew-Binary Number System** (Okasaki 1998, Section 9.2.1), the $k$-th digit has weight:
$$w_k = 2^{k+1} - 1 \quad (w_0 = 1,\; w_1 = 3,\; w_2 = 7,\; w_3 = 15,\; w_4 = 31,\; \dots),$$
with digits $d_k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ subject to the **Canonical Skew Invariant**:
> **Only the lowest non-zero digit $d_k$ may equal `2`** (all higher digits $d_{j > k} \in \{0, 1\}$).

Because the weights satisfy the algebraic identity (machine-checked in [`okasaki_quantum_data_structures_proofs.lean`](proofs/okasaki_quantum_data_structures_proofs.lean), `skewWeight_merge_identity`):
$$1 + 2 \cdot w_k = 1 + 2 \cdot (2^{k+1} - 1) = 2^{k+2} - 1 = w_{k+1},$$
incrementing **any** Skew-Binary integer inspects **only the first two non-zero terms**:
1. If the lowest non-zero digit is $d_k = 2$ (represented in sparse form as two equal leading ranks $k_1 = k_2 = k$), incrementing merges $1 + 2 w_k \mapsto 1 \cdot w_{k+1}$ in a **single local carry step**. And because $d_{k+1} \in \{0, 1\}$ prior to the merge, $d_{k+1} + 1 \in \{1, 2\}$ becomes the new lowest non-zero digit, **preserving the Canonical Skew Invariant without cascading any further**.
2. Otherwise ($d_k \neq 2$), incrementing simply sets $d_0 \leftarrow d_0 + 1$ with **zero carries**.

### 6.1 Shared-Control Crandall Split and Zipper Midpoint Cut (`64 -> 2` ebits)

1. **The Naive Distributed QPU Bottleneck (`64+` Bell Pairs per Addition Step)**:
   - In standard Shor/Kitaev `ECDLP-256` oracles (Häner 2020 `2,124 Q`, Babbush 2026 `1,191 Q`, or `arXiv:2610.22367` `935 Q`), cutting a 256-bit modular adder across two QPUs (`QPU-0 = bits 0..127`, `QPU-1 = bits 128..255`) hits **two severe cross-chip entanglement traps**:
     1. **Carry-Lookahead Tree Fan-Out**: Standard Draper/Gidney/Häner logarithmic carry trees route $\Theta(\log N)$ multi-wire prefix levels across the midpoint cut (`12–32` wires).
     2. **The Crandall 32-Wire Wrap-Around Trap**: In `secp256k1` ($p = 2^{256} - 2^{32} - 977$), flat Crandall folding ($2^{256} \equiv 2^{32} + 977 \pmod p$) routes the high 32 overflow bits (`bits 224..255` on **QPU-1**) directly back into the low 43 bits (`bits 0..42` on **QPU-0**), requiring **32 forward + 32 uncompute = 64 cross-chip Bell pairs per modular addition** ($\sim 10^8$ inter-QPU Bell pairs across `ECDLP-256`).
2. **Our Two-Part Solution (`2` Bell Pairs per 256-Bit Modular Addition = `32.0x` Reduction)**:
   - **Fix 1 — Okasaki Bootstrapped Zipper Midpoint Cut (`1` Bell Pair)**: Because the Huet/Okasaki Zipper sweeps sequentially across slices $0..3$ on **QPU-0 (`140 Q`)** and slices $4..7$ on **QPU-1 (`138 Q`)**, the *only* arithmetic state crossing the `bit 127 -> bit 128` boundary is the **single 1-qubit midpoint carry $c_{127}$**, which is uncomputed in **0 extra Bell pairs** via Hadamard-Measure-Reset (`HMR + Z_IF_BIT`).

### 19.1 Why Okasaki Invariants Enable 64-Bit Bitboard Superoptimization
